# Hnefatafl provenance

## Historical game family

**Game:** Hnefatafl / tafl family.

Tafl games are historically attested in northern Europe, including Viking-age archaeological and literary evidence, but no complete Viking-age Hnefatafl rules text survives. Chess Atlas therefore separates the historical game identity from the modern rules reconstruction used to make it playable.

## Implemented profile

Profile id: `hnefatafl-fetlar-2007`

**Rules attribution:** Fetlar Hnefatafl Panel, Shetland, 2007.

This modern reconstruction uses an 11×11 board, 24 attackers, 12 defenders and one king. Attackers move first. Pieces move orthogonally like rooks. The king alone may occupy the throne and four corner refuge squares; pieces may cross an empty throne. Ordinary pieces use custodial capture, with restricted squares acting as hostile supports under the Fetlar rules. The defenders win by taking the king to a corner. The attackers win by capturing the king or by enclosing the entire defending force so escape is impossible. A player with no legal move loses. Repetition/impossible endings are draws in the published rules; repetition-state adjudication is not yet automated by this engine.

The king requires four-sided capture away from the board edge. Beside the throne, three attackers plus the throne suffice. The board edge is not a hostile capture surface.

## Reconstruction boundary

These rules must not be described as the recovered rules of Viking-age Hnefatafl. They are a named 2007 reconstruction informed by the tafl tradition and by later documented Tablut. Copenhagen Hnefatafl (2012) is a distinct later ruleset with additions such as shieldwall and exit-fort rules and should remain a separate profile.
