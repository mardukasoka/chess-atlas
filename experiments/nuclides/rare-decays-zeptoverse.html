Rare Decays & Zeptometre Probes

Experimental evidence for exceptionally rare processes can constrain physics at short distance scales without directly imaging those distances. This is a linked particle-physics observation, not an exotic atomic bound state.

Neutral beauty meson → muon pair

Process: B⁰ → μ⁺ μ⁻ (B⁰d decay)

Experiment
LHCb, CERN
Evidence status
Reported evidence; not a five-sigma discovery
Interpretation
Flavour-changing neutral-current decay; highly suppressed in the Standard Model. New heavy particles could affect its branching fraction through virtual contributions.
Atlas classification
Experimental particle-decay observation; cross-linked to hadrons, leptons, flavour physics and beyond-Standard-Model constraints.

Reported in November 2026 after a decades-long search. Numerical significance and branching fraction should be checked against the primary LHCb analysis before being entered as authoritative measured fields.

What does “zeptoverse” mean?

One zeptometre (zm) = 10⁻²¹ metres, or 10⁻⁶ femtometres. Rare-decay measurements can indirectly constrain high-energy phenomena sometimes described using this scale; they do not establish a new particle, an observed zeptometre-sized object, or a directly measured spatial resolution of 1 zm.

Scale comparison: atomic ~10⁻¹⁰ m; nuclear ~10⁻¹⁵ m; zeptometre 10⁻²¹ m; Planck length ~1.6 × 10⁻³⁵ m.
