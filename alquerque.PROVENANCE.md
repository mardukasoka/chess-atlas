# Alquerque de Doze provenance

## Historical evidence

Alquerque de Doze is described in Alfonso X's *Libro de los Juegos* (1283), fols. 91r–92r. The Digital Ludeme Project records this as a contemporary rules description with high confidence: a 5×5 board of intersecting lines with diagonals, twelve pieces per player, movement to an adjacent connected point, capture by hopping an opposing piece, and the objective of capturing all opposing pieces.

The game tradition is older than the Castilian manuscript and is associated with the Arabic-speaking world, but Chess Atlas does not assign a precise invention date that the evidence cannot support.

## Playable Atlas profile

- **Profile id:** `alquerque-alfonso-1283`
- **Attribution:** Alfonso X, *Libro de los Juegos*
- **Date:** 1283 CE
- **Evidence class:** contemporary textual/illustrated rules evidence
- **Board:** 25 intersections on the characteristic Alquerque line graph
- **Pieces:** 12 per player
- **Securely implemented rules:** adjacent movement along a drawn line; hop capture over an adjacent opposing man to the empty connected point beyond; victory by eliminating all opposing men

The board graph has orthogonal connections at every point and diagonal connections at thirteen intersections, matching the characteristic repeated Alquerque line pattern rather than treating the board as a fully diagonal 5×5 grid.

## Deliberately excluded from this profile

Later or reconstructed conventions such as compulsory capture, huffing a piece that fails to capture, compulsory continuation of multiple jumps, and winning by immobilisation are not silently added to the Alfonso profile. They may be introduced later as separately attributed rules profiles if supported and tested.

This distinction lets Chess Atlas present a playable medieval rules core while keeping later reconstruction choices visible rather than retrojecting them into 1283.
