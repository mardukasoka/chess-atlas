# Ludus Latrunculorum provenance

The complete Roman rules of Ludus Latrunculorum do not survive. Chess Atlas therefore separates the game's ancient historical position from the date and author of the modern rules reconstruction used for play.

## Primary playable profile

- **Game tradition:** Ludus Latrunculorum
- **Atlas historical position:** Roman antiquity; attested by the late Republic / early Imperial period
- **Playable reconstruction:** Ulrich Schädler, 1994
- **Profile id:** `latrunculi-schadler-1994`
- **Status:** playable reconstruction
- **Uncertainty:** high for the lost ancient rules; the implemented profile is intentionally attributed rather than labelled canonical

The implementation follows the Schädler 1994 reconstruction profile used by modern archaeoludological references: alternating placement, orthogonal one-step movement, leaps over occupied men including multiple leaps, custodial immobilisation, release when an enclosing enemy is itself surrounded, removal of an enclosed man on a later turn only while the enclosure remains effective and its enclosing men remain free, and defeat when reduced to one man.

Archaeological boards attributed to Roman gaming vary in dimensions. The engine's default 8×8 board is therefore an implementation profile, not a claim that all Roman Latrunculi was played on a canonical 8×8 board.

## Alternative reconstructions

Other modern reconstructions are kept conceptually separate. They should be added as their own attributed profiles only when implemented and tested, rather than mixing features such as a *dux* or different capture rules into the Schädler profile.
