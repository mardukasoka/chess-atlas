# Early board-game network: evidence boundary

This block adds Mehen and Fifty-Eight Holes as archaeological game profiles without presenting uncertain reconstructions as ancient fact.

## Mehen

Securely represented here: Egyptian spiral serpent board; lion/lioness pieces and marbles associated with the game in Old Kingdom evidence. The ancient rules and scoring system are not preserved, so this profile is not marked playable.

Primary museum references:
- British Museum EA66216
- Metropolitan Museum of Art, *Board Games from Ancient Egypt and the Near East*

## Fifty-Eight Holes / Hounds and Jackals

Securely represented here: paired peg tracks, two sets of five pieces, race-game structure, Middle Kingdom Egyptian examples, and Bronze Age distribution into the Near East including Anatolia.

Howard Carter and Lord Carnarvon proposed a playable interpretation in 1912, but the complete ancient rules and the original Egyptian name are unknown. The Atlas therefore records that reconstruction historically but does not silently adopt it as the ancient ruleset.

Primary museum references:
- Metropolitan Museum 26.7.1287a-k
- Metropolitan Museum 12.182.72
- Metropolitan Museum 36.70.37g

## Atlas principle

`attested object -> archaeological interpretation -> named reconstruction -> playable implementation`

Each transition must remain explicit. Trade-route or cultural diffusion claims should be attached separately to dated findspots rather than inferred solely from visual similarity.
